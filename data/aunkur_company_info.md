## Company Overview

### What Aunkur is

The IC memo describes Aunkur as a farm analytics engine for small farmers that uses Artificial Intelligence of Things (AIoT). It combines rapid soil testing devices, weather stations, predictive models, and agronomic intelligence to improve farming decisions, support sustainable practices, reduce fertilizer use, increase productivity, and connect farmers with agriculture ecosystem actors. The memo states a starting farmer subscription price of USD 2.50 per month per acre and B2B analytics packages starting at USD 63 per 1,000 API hits.

*Source pages: 1*

### Vision and data-bank strategy

Aunkur's stated vision is to build a large agricultural data bank in the Global South by capturing hyperlocal soil, weather, crop, and sustainability intelligence. The memo frames this data layer as infrastructure for future agri-finance, carbon markets, regenerative supply chains, and broader agricultural decision-making. Aunkur says it does not primarily sell devices to farmers; it scales farm-level analytics and data-derived insights across the agricultural value chain.

*Source pages: 1, 3*

## Problem

### Smallholder data gap

The memo states that more than 600 million smallholder farmers globally, including about 7 million in Bangladesh, face declining yields, soil degradation, rising input costs, and limited access to timely, localized, actionable data. It argues that lack of farm-level soil, microclimatic weather, and sustainable-practice information causes inefficient decisions and contributes to an under-accounted carbon footprint at the base of the food supply chain.

*Source pages: 1*

### Soil testing bottleneck

Traditional laboratory chemical soil testing is described as taking 15-20 days, which the memo says is too slow for many cropping decisions. Satellite-only soil diagnostics are described as insufficiently precise for farms under 2.5 acres, a common smallholder plot size in Asia and Africa. Aunkur's position is that small fragmented plots need ground-level, high-resolution soil testing to generate actionable recommendations.

*Source pages: 1, 2*

### Hyperlocal weather blind spots

The memo says satellite weather models can miss microclimatic variation that matters for small farms and varied terrain. It advocates combining satellite information with localized ground observatories so that farm-level weather data can support irrigation decisions, pest and disease risk alerts, and planting-window recommendations.

*Source pages: 2*

### Extension-service capacity gap

The memo states that Bangladesh has roughly one extension officer for every 2,500 farmers, making personalized and timely human advisory difficult. It proposes decentralizing extension services through automated channels such as outbound dialer calls and text messages, semi-automated smartphone applications, and local community-level support.

*Source pages: 2*

### Disconnected B2B agriculture ecosystem

The memo argues that banks, insurers, input vendors, and buyers often lack verified granular field data. Without such data, these actors may struggle to assess risk, design tailored products, plan demand, or support transparent sustainable sourcing. Aunkur positions detailed agronomic data as a way to improve decision-making across these stakeholders.

*Source pages: 2*

### Strategic insight and why now

The memo characterizes the agriculture information gap as a USD 298 billion critical data-infrastructure void within a USD 2.5 trillion-plus industry. It says agri-input inflation, climate instability, the shift toward regenerative agriculture, and rising rural internet penetration create a timely opportunity for data-driven agriculture. The memo cites APAC rural internet penetration increasing from 42.6% to 50.4%.

*Source pages: 2*

*Retrieval note: Treat the market-size and internet-penetration figures as claims stated in the memo, not independently verified facts.*

## Solution

### Meaning of Aunkur and core workflow

The memo says "Aunkur" is a Bengali word associated with sprouting, symbolizing new beginnings, potential, and transformation. Operationally, Aunkur combines proprietary IoT devices such as soil testers and weather stations with predictive models and agro-scientific intelligence. The engine produces real-time, site-specific and crop-specific analytics, which are delivered through digital channels including AI-triggered outbound calls and text messages.

*Source pages: 3*

### Farmer activity tracking and ecosystem linkage

Aunkur says it tracks farmer activity using AI-prompted IVR calls plus limited in-person visits by franchise agents. These interactions support farmer monitoring, targeted lead generation, and direct farmer linkage for B2B partners. The memo frames the platform as a scalable way to decentralize agricultural extension services and provide real-time farm analytics across B2B and B2G use cases.

*Source pages: 3*

## B2C Product

### Digital farmer profiling and Smart Farmer Card

Aunkur begins farmer engagement with on-ground sensitization sessions, then registers interested farmers into dynamic, consent-based digital profiles that accumulate agronomic data over time. Each registered farmer receives an NFC-enabled Smart Farmer Card. The memo positions the card as a digital identity that helps traditionally offline rural users access finance, agricultural inputs, services, and other parts of the agri-value chain.

*Source pages: 4*

### Rapid soil testing and AI farm planning

The memo states that Aunkur's proprietary AI engine can deliver crop-suitability and input recommendations in 5-7 minutes using rapid soil diagnostics. It interprets 11 physio-chemical soil properties to guide decisions on seeds, fertilizers, and pesticides. It also generates crop- and site-specific budgeting insights that financial institutions can use when setting agricultural loan ceilings.

*Source pages: 4*

### Hyperlocal weather intelligence

Aunkur deploys portable mini-weather stations that the memo says collect village-level agrometeorological data across 13 parameters. The data supports early warnings for pest and disease outbreaks and farm-specific agronomic countermeasures delivered through voice calls and text messages in local languages. The memo also says this data could support crop-insurance risk scoring and references a USD 525 million untapped market.

*Source pages: 4*

*Retrieval note: The USD 525M figure is a source claim about a potential crop-insurance market.*

### Training, franchise network and remote advisory

Aunkur says it equips local youth as franchise partners with tools for soil testing, advisory, and farmer training. It works with government extension departments on Training-of-Trainers programs to localize expertise. Franchise outlets serve as data-collection and knowledge hubs, while the AI engine and outbound-dialer system push timely advisories based on farmer practices.

*Source pages: 4*

### Preferential access to products and services

Using farmer profiles and real-time demand signals, Aunkur says it creates digitized customer personas for B2B input and service providers. Farmers can request products through smartphones or local franchise outlets. Transactions are recorded through Smart Farmer Cards and point-of-sale systems to support traceable, need-based fulfillment from partner suppliers.

*Source pages: 4*

## B2B Product

### Bank KYC, credit scoring and monitoring

For banks and financial institutions, Aunkur provides agronomic data infrastructure through digital dashboards and API integration. The memo describes KYC and credit-scoring tools integrated into digital loan-documentation workflows, with real-time insights into credit risk, farm activities, and possible signs of default or repayment delay. The stated objective is to make small agricultural loans more scalable and evidence-based.

*Source pages: 5*

### Closed-loop loan disbursement

The memo describes a closed-loop agricultural financing model in which a large portion of a sanctioned loan can be released directly to participating input vendors when a farmer makes approved purchases, while the remaining funds are available as cash for essential overheads. Aunkur says this design improves traceability, reduces misuse of funds, and strengthens the integrity of agricultural finance.

*Source pages: 5*

### Banking outcome claims

The memo claims that digitizing and streamlining agricultural lending with Aunkur has helped banking partners reduce capital mobilization costs by up to 45%, reduce non-performing loans by 50%, and nearly double processing efficiency. These figures are presented as company-reported impact or performance claims within the memo.

*Source pages: 5*

*Retrieval note: Use wording such as "the memo claims" or "Aunkur reports" when answering with these outcomes.*

### Demand intelligence and payment assurance for input vendors

Aunkur says rapid soil tests and season-specific farm recommendations reveal what inputs farmers are likely to need, when they need them, and in what quantities. This information is converted into SKU-level demand forecasts and high-intent customer leads for input vendors. Partner vendors use digital PoS devices; registered farmers tap Smart Farmer Cards to purchase pre-approved inputs, and the system can trigger bank fund release. The memo states that vendor payments are ensured within 7 working days.

*Source pages: 5*

### Current and planned B2B analytics coverage

At the time of the memo, Aunkur says its dedicated local data infrastructures are focused on banking institutions and input vendors. Similar analytics dashboards for insurance companies and development organizations are described as being tested in-house. The broader product vision is a full-cycle data system spanning the agricultural value chain.

*Source pages: 5, 6*

## Customer Personas

### Progressive rural farmer persona

The memo's ideal B2C persona is a progressive rural farmer in Bangladesh, around 55 years old, with basic to primary education and typically 0.5-5 acres through ownership, leasing, or sharecropping. It lists average earnings of USD 140-235 per month per acre, average spending of about USD 125 per month per acre, and a 10-12% gross margin. Advice usually comes from peer farmers and input sellers rather than government agencies. Capital often comes from local lenders at 40-110% annual rates or microcredit at 25-35%. The persona is described as open to new solutions when results are demonstrated, but highly sensitive to risk and cost.

*Source pages: 6, 7*

### Farmer production, labor and market constraints

The target farmer persona is described as relying heavily on manual labor for land preparation, sowing, weeding, and harvesting, with seasonal labor costs rising by as much as 25% before harvest. Irrigation often depends on local machine-service vendors. The memo says small harvest volumes make direct market access uneconomical, so farmers commonly sell through intermediaries and hoarders. Cropping decisions often follow traditional cycles and are influenced by peers, input dealers, or unreliable market signals.

*Source pages: 6, 7*

### Second-generation private bank persona

The memo's ideal B2B bank persona is a second-generation private bank focused on agriculture and high-value crops. Typical agricultural loan tickets are listed as USD 1,000-2,500 without collateral and USD 2,500-5,000 with minimal collateral, usually for six months. The memo lists cost of funds of 4% per year for cereal crops, oilseeds, and spices, and 10.25% for other crops. The core problem is that banks may have strong digital infrastructure elsewhere but lack suitable small-farmer documentation, real-time monitoring, farmer-side data entry, and scalable distribution for agricultural loans.

*Source pages: 6, 7*

### Local retail input vendor persona

The memo describes a local retail input vendor as a sole or mixed distributor of major input brands selling through an offline shop. It states that about 70% of customers buy first and pay later, typically after the season, with typical sales margins of 10-18% and sales volume of USD 500-850 per acre per season depending on crop. Core problems include guesswork in seasonal restocking, only about 40% regular customers, risk of overstocking or missed sales, delayed farmer payments, and about 5% non-recovery of payments.

*Source pages: 7, 8*

## Expansion Strategy

### Future ICP and B2G expansion

The memo says future B2C customers may include large contract farmers. Potential B2B customers may expand to machine-service providers, bulk buyers, research institutions, and INGOs. It also identifies governments in developing Asian and African countries and selected Middle Eastern markets as difficult but high-potential B2G partners. The proposed de-risking approach is to run targeted pilots to calibrate AI to local conditions and use blended support from impact investors and agriculture-development organizations to reduce pilot cost and risk.

*Source pages: 8*

## Revenue

### Revenue model overview

Aunkur describes two main revenue streams. B2C revenue is charged to farmers for farm analytics and ecosystem access. B2B revenue is charged to agriculture ecosystem partners for access to different layers of farm data analytics through dashboards and APIs. The business model is therefore based primarily on monetizing analytics and data access rather than selling field devices to farmers.

*Source pages: 8*

## Capital Strategy

### Why Aunkur owns devices instead of selling them

The memo argues that Aunkur's analytics depend on high-quality AIoT devices deployed in the field and strategically owned by the company. It says selling devices to farmers would either exclude much of the 600 million-plus smallholder market if quality were maintained, force hardware quality down to reach low price points, or compromise technical integrity for hardware margins. Aunkur therefore treats device capex as a strategic moat that supports data monetization.

*Source pages: 16*

### ROE and ROA projections

The page 17 chart projects Return on Equity (ROE) of 9% in FY 2026, 23% in FY 2027, 40% in FY 2028, 75% in FY 2029, 68% in FY 2030, and 62% in FY 2031. It projects Return on Assets (ROA) of 1%, 2%, 4%, 20%, 30%, and 37% over the same years. The memo interprets the rising ROA as stronger utilization of assets such as soil testers, weather stations, and digital platforms, and the gap between ROE and ROA as an intended effect of leverage and strategic equity deployment.

*Source pages: 17*
